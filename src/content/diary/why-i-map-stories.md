---
title: "Why I draw a story before I write it"
description: "A story map reveals not only what happens, but what changes inside the character."
pubDate: 2026-10-24
tags: ["story craft", "process"]
featured: false
---

I used to think a story began with sentences.

Now I often begin with a map.

On the first line, I draw the outer journey: the places a character visits, the choices they make, and the events they cannot reverse.

On the second line, I draw the inner journey: alone, afraid, found, longing, loving, waiting, grieving, carrying.

The outer line tells me what happens. The inner line tells me why anyone should care.

Mapping also exposes weak scenes. If a character travels a long distance but does not change, I may have movement without meaning. If the emotional line jumps without a threshold, I may be asking the audience to feel something the story has not earned.

For my current story, one sentence holds the map together:

> Do not be afraid. I am here.

At first it is rescue. Later it becomes love. In the end, it becomes memory.

The map does not replace writing. It gives the writing somewhere to go.
